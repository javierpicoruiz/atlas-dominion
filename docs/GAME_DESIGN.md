# ATLAS DOMINION — Game Design Document v0.1

## 1. High concept
ATLAS DOMINION is a mobile-first persistent real-time grand-strategy game played on a world map. A normal campaign lasts about seven real days.

The player controls cities, resource sites, transport routes, ports, armies and research. Orders continue to resolve while the app is closed. Expansion is constrained by logistics, city morale and economic sustainability rather than only by military strength.

The central design principle is:

> Conquering territory is easy. Keeping it supplied, stable and productive is hard.

The first implementation should be single-player versus AI and installable as a PWA. Multiplayer can be added later without redesigning the core simulation.

---

## 2. Core loop

1. Inspect world state.
2. Secure food, money, strategic resources and supply routes.
3. Develop cities and extraction sites.
4. Recruit and position armies.
5. Scout neighbouring regions.
6. Attack cities, mines, ports and transport corridors.
7. Stabilise newly conquered population.
8. Research technologies and unlock stronger units.
9. Adapt to shortages, rebellions, enemy offensives and market prices.
10. Finish the seven-day campaign with the strongest strategic position.

The player should be able to make meaningful progress in 5–10 minute sessions, while long actions resolve over tens of minutes or hours.

---

## 3. World structure

### 3.1 Map
The visual centre of the app is a full-screen interactive world map.

The map contains four strategic object types:

- **Cities** — population, tax income, consumption, morale, infrastructure, recruitment.
- **Resource sites** — mines, oilfields, farms and rare-material deposits.
- **Ports** — maritime transport, imports/exports and global market access.
- **Routes** — roads/rail corridors and sea lanes connecting strategic nodes.

The underlying simulation should use a graph. Nodes have latitude/longitude for rendering; edges define legal movement and logistics.

This is preferable to free movement because it makes supply lines, chokepoints, ports and artillery range understandable on a phone.

### 3.2 Territory
Territory is represented by zones around nodes rather than thousands of individual map provinces.

A normal world seed should target:
- 80–120 cities.
- 35–60 resource sites.
- 25–40 major ports.
- 180–300 land connections.
- 50–90 sea-lane connections.

Cities can also be ports.

### 3.3 City classes
| Class | Population | Building slots | Base tax | Base consumption | Strategic role |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Town | 0.2–1m | 3 | Low | Low | frontier / minor hub |
| Regional city | 1–3m | 4 | Medium | Medium | recruitment / trade |
| Major city | 3–8m | 5 | High | High | industry / research |
| Metropolis | 8m+ | 6 | Very high | Very high | capital-scale prize |

Population is an abstract strategic value, not a literal census simulation.

## 4. Resources

The launch version uses six resources.

### Money
Obtained from taxes, ports, trade and certain events.
Used for every building, unit, research project and maintenance cost.

### Food
Produced by agricultural sites and some cities.
Consumed continuously by cities and armies.
Food shortages are one of the fastest ways to destroy morale.

### Iron
Extracted from mines.
Used primarily for artillery, fortifications, industrial buildings and mechanised units.

### Oil
Extracted from oilfields.
Used by mechanised, naval and air units.
Late-game military power becomes impossible without it.

### Energy
Generated by power infrastructure and some resource nodes.
Consumed by advanced cities, industry and research.

### Rare materials
Low-volume strategic resource.
Used for advanced electronics, radar, helicopters, drones and top research tiers.

Resources are stored globally only when connected to the player's logistics network. Isolated cities maintain a small local stockpile.

---

## 5. Economy and city consumption

Each city has:
- Population
- Tax base
- Food demand
- Energy demand
- Local stockpile
- Infrastructure level
- Morale
- Stability
- Damage / city HP
- Buildings
- Garrison
- Supply connection
- Occupation age
- Owner
- Core/foreign status

### 5.1 Consumption
Every simulation tick the city consumes food and energy.

Example hourly demand:
`foodDemand = populationMillions × 10 × developmentMultiplier`
`energyDemand = populationMillions × 5 × developmentMultiplier`

Large, developed cities are therefore powerful but expensive.

### 5.2 Tax income
`tax = population × development × moraleMultiplier × stabilityMultiplier`

A newly occupied metropolis can initially cost more than it earns.

### 5.3 Shortages
If required resources are unavailable:
- first 30 min: small morale loss;
- 30–120 min: strong morale loss;
- 2h+: very strong morale loss, production penalties and unrest;
- prolonged shortage: population loss, desertion and rebellion risk.

---

## 6. Morale, stability and rebellion

### 6.1 Morale
Morale is 0–100.

Factors:
- food and energy fulfilment;
- taxes / economic pressure;
- stability;
- recent conquest;
- nearby enemy armies;
- military defeats;
- garrison;
- buildings;
- special events.

### 6.2 Stability
Stability is the long-term control score of a city.

After conquest:
- stability begins around 20–30;
- rises slowly while the city is supplied and peaceful;
- can be increased by garrison, administration and civilian investment;
- falls from shortages, bombardment, enemy propaganda/event effects and repeated recapture.

Suggested natural recovery:
- +0.5 to +1.5 stability per real hour depending on conditions.

### 6.3 Morale formula
A useful initial approximation:

`targetMorale = 20 + 0.45*stability + 25*resourceFulfilment + buildingBonus - warPenalty`

Current morale converges gradually toward target morale rather than jumping instantly.

### 6.4 Rebellion
A city at <20 morale enters **critical unrest**.

To prevent annoying instant flips, rebellion occurs only if:
- morale remains <20 for 45 continuous minutes; and
- no emergency stabilisation succeeds.

When rebellion fires:
- city leaves the current state;
- a new rebel state is generated;
- local stockpile is retained;
- militia/guerrilla infantry spawn based on population;
- stability resets;
- nearby low-morale cities may receive a temporary unrest modifier.

Large cities should be dangerous to lose.

---

## 7. City health

Cities have **City Integrity** (HP), representing infrastructure, defensive positions and administration.

Typical values:
- Town: 400 HP
- Regional: 650 HP
- Major: 900 HP
- Metropolis: 1200 HP

Damage is caused primarily by artillery, air attack and direct assault.

Recovery depends on morale:
- 80–100 morale: fast repair
- 50–79: normal repair
- 20–49: slow repair
- <20: no natural repair

Example:
`repairPerHour = maxHP * baseRepairRate * morale/100`

A damaged city:
- earns less money;
- recruits more slowly;
- has lower supply throughput;
- is easier to capture.

### Essential
**Barracks**
- L1 infantry
- L2 cavalry / improved infantry
- L3 elite infantry / specialisation

**Arsenal**
- artillery
- anti-air
- later heavy weapons

**Depot**
- storage
- supply throughput
- larger local reserve

**Fortifications**
- increases city defence
- reduces bombardment damage

**Market**
- tax and economic output
- lowers some civilian shortage penalties

### Advanced
**University**
- generates research points
- required for advanced technologies

**Factory**
- artillery production bonus
- mechanised units
- advanced upgrades

**Rail Hub**
- major movement and logistics bonus on connected land routes

**Hospital**
- faster unit recovery
- improved city recovery

**Airfield**
- aircraft and helicopters
- reconnaissance radius

**Power Plant**
- energy generation
- development bonus

**Port**
- only coastal
- sea transport
- world-market access
- import/export capacity

Buildings have construction times from ~30 minutes to 6 real hours.

## 9. Military units

All units use:
- manpower abstraction
- money
- strategic resources
- recruitment time
- movement speed
- upkeep
- combat stats
- supply requirement

### Launch units

| Unit | Role | Cost | Recruit | Speed | Range | Relative strength |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Militia | emergency defence | 70 | 8m | 25 km/h | 0 | very low |
| Infantry | cheap mass army | 120 | 15m | 35 km/h | 0 | low/medium |
| Cavalry | fast attack/scouting | 320 | 30m | 60 km/h | 0 | medium |
| Field Artillery | siege/ranged | 520 | 45m | 18 km/h | 150 km | high vs city |
| Motorised Infantry | mobile line unit | 480 | 60m | 75 km/h | 0 | medium/high |
| Tank | breakthrough | 900 | 90m | 65 km/h | 0 | high |
| Anti-Air | air defence | 600 | 70m | 45 km/h | 100 km | specialist |
| Helicopter | rapid strike | 1100 | 120m | 220 km/h | 80 km | high |
| Fighter | air superiority | 1400 | 150m | strategic | 350 km | specialist |
| Bomber | city/army strike | 1700 | 180m | strategic | 450 km | very high strike |
| Transport Ship | moves land units | 700 | 90m | 30 km/h | n/a | non-combat |
| Destroyer | escort/blockade | 1300 | 150m | 50 km/h | 120 km | naval |

Numbers are balance placeholders.

### 9.1 Unit upgrades
Units gain technology levels rather than being replaced every few hours.

Possible upgrade dimensions:
- weapons;
- armour;
- mobility;
- logistics;
- training;
- reconnaissance.

Each upgrade should create a 10–20% advantage, not a 2× power jump.

## 10. Combat

Combat is resolved continuously while opposing forces occupy the same contested node or engage through range.

### Direct combat
Every combat interval:
1. determine effective attack;
2. apply terrain;
3. apply morale;
4. apply supply;
5. apply technology;
6. apply counter bonuses;
7. distribute casualties;
8. update unit morale.

No pure rock-paper-scissors system. Counters should matter without making units useless.

Examples:
- infantry performs well in cities;
- cavalry/motorised forces excel at manoeuvre;
- tanks dominate open ground but need oil and logistics;
- artillery is excellent at siege but weak if caught;
- anti-air protects against aircraft;
- helicopters are fast but vulnerable to anti-air.

### Capture
A city is captured when:
- defending field army is defeated/withdrawn;
- attacker has a direct-presence unit in the city;
- city resistance/capture meter reaches zero.

Bombardment alone cannot claim territory.

---

## 11. Artillery and ranged attack

Artillery is intentionally slow but strategically important.

Rules:
- can fire at enemy armies/cities inside range;
- requires supply;
- has a firing cooldown;
- moving interrupts fire;
- deals strong infrastructure damage;
- is vulnerable to fast units;
- becomes much stronger with reconnaissance.

Range is calculated from map distance, but valid targets should normally be connected/visible strategic nodes to keep mobile UI readable.

---

## 12. Movement

Movement is timestamp based.

`arrivalTime = distance / effectiveSpeed × terrain × supplyModifier`

When several unit types move together, stack speed is limited by the slowest unit unless slower units are detached.

Examples:
- neighbouring urban corridor: infantry 20–45m
- long regional move: 1–3h
- continental relocation: several hours
- intercontinental sea movement: 4–12h

This pacing supports a seven-day campaign.

---

## 13. Logistics

Logistics is one of the game's main strategic systems.

Every controlled node attempts to trace a path to a supplied city/depot/port.

Supply network capacity depends on:
- roads/rail;
- depots;
- ports;
- distance;
- enemy control;
- blockades;
- infrastructure damage.

Out-of-supply armies:
- first 30m: warning;
- after 30m: combat penalty;
- after 2h: severe combat/movement penalty;
- prolonged: attrition and morale loss.

This prevents unlimited deep rushes.

---

## 14. Ports and global trade

Ports have:
- level;
- trade capacity;
- local storage;
- sea transport capacity;
- blockade state.

A supplied, unblocked port grants access to the **World Market**.

The market is abstract external trade.

Resources can be:
- bought immediately at ask price;
- sold at bid price.

Prices vary according to:
- global scarcity;
- campaign day;
- player/AI aggregate supply and demand;
- scripted shocks.

Imports should be expensive enough that mines remain strategically important.

Example base price indexes:
- Food 8
- Iron 14
- Oil 22
- Energy 10
- Rare materials 45

A blockade cuts or strongly reduces market capacity.

## 16. Fog of war and intelligence

Visible:
- owned nodes;
- allied/intelligence-shared nodes;
- areas near friendly units.

Unknown enemy information is displayed in confidence bands:
- exact;
- estimated;
- stale;
- unknown.

Scouting sources:
- cavalry;
- aircraft;
- radar;
- adjacent observation;
- spies/events in a later version.

This prevents the map from becoming a perfect-information spreadsheet.

## 17. Diplomacy

For v1 AI campaigns:
- war;
- peace;
- non-aggression pact;
- trade agreement;
- military access.

Later:
- alliances;
- shared intelligence;
- coalitions;
- player multiplayer diplomacy.

AI should remember aggression and broken agreements.

## 18. AI personalities

Each AI faction receives a doctrine:
- **Expansionist** — prioritises nearby weak cities.
- **Fortress** — builds defence and counterattacks.
- **Industrialist** — prioritises economy, resources and mechanised forces.
- **Technocrat** — research-heavy, delayed military spike.
- **Trader** — ports, cash and imports.
- **Raider** — fast armies, supply disruption.

AI evaluates actions every 5–15 minutes rather than every tick.

Utility factors:
- target military strength;
- resource value;
- population;
- supply distance;
- relation;
- strategic chokepoint value;
- current shortages;
- enemy vulnerability.

---

## 19. Dynamic events

Events should create decisions, not random punishment.

Examples:
- mine accident;
- harvest surplus;
- port strike;
- epidemic;
- refugee wave;
- military desertion;
- industrial boom;
- fuel shortage;
- separatist movement;
- university breakthrough;
- trade embargo;
- storm closing sea route.

Events can be accepted/mitigated using money/resources.

## 20. Campaign pacing — 7 real days

### Day 1 — Consolidation
- basic economy
- infantry/cavalry
- local expansion
- first resource fights

### Day 2 — Regional power
- artillery
- fortifications
- first serious wars
- universities begin to matter

### Day 3 — Industrialisation
- factories
- motorisation
- stronger logistics

### Day 4 — Mechanised war
- tanks
- rail hubs
- long fronts

### Day 5 — Air age
- aircraft/helicopters
- reconnaissance
- rapid-response warfare

### Day 6 — Global crisis
- resource scarcity
- blockades
- mature alliances/wars
- large offensives

### Day 7 — Endgame
- final objectives
- score pressure
- unstable overextended empires can collapse

Research time and costs should naturally create this curve rather than hard-locking all features by day.

---

## 21. Victory

Campaign ends after 168 real hours unless domination is achieved earlier.

Score categories:
- controlled population;
- economic output;
- resource security;
- military strength;
- technology;
- average morale;
- strategic objectives.

A domination victory may trigger if one faction controls, for example:
- 65% of world population value; and
- 50% of designated strategic capitals;
- sustained for 3 real hours.

Exact thresholds are tuning values.

---

## 22. New-game setup

Player selects:
- starting city or region;
- difficulty;
- world seed;
- AI count;
- optional campaign speed.

Recommended default:
- 10 major AI states;
- 20–30 neutral/minor states;
- player starts with 2–4 connected cities;
- 1 nearby resource site;
- small infantry force;
- enough food/money for the first few hours.

Starting locations should be balanced by total nearby value rather than identical geography.

## 24. UX principles

- Most common action: maximum 2 taps from map.
- Large touch targets.
- Bottom sheets rather than desktop sidebars.
- Long processes show exact finish time.
- Every cost shows both absolute cost and "hours remaining at current burn".
- No hidden resource drains.
- Confirmation only for destructive/high-cost actions.
- Offline state catches up instantly when app is reopened.

---

## 25. Simulation architecture

Use a deterministic simulation driven by timestamps.

Core entities:
- GameState
- Faction
- City
- ResourceNode
- Port
- Route
- Army
- UnitGroup
- Building
- ResearchProject
- Market
- Event

Important principle:
Do **not** run a background JavaScript timer continuously to represent seven days.

Instead store:
- action start time;
- action completion time;
- last simulation timestamp.

When app opens:
1. calculate elapsed time;
2. replay/summarise scheduled events;
3. resolve production/consumption;
4. resolve movement/combat;
5. update morale/stability;
6. let AI take due decisions.

This is robust on mobile browsers.

---

## 26. Save system

v1:
- IndexedDB for game state;
- compressed periodic snapshot;
- autosave after every command;
- export/import save JSON.

Optional cloud later:
- Supabase authentication + database.

The game should be fully playable offline after assets are cached, except external map tiles if used.

## 27. Technical stack

Recommended for the same easy GitHub workflow as a small web app:

- Vite
- React
- TypeScript
- Zustand for UI/game state orchestration
- MapLibre GL JS for the map
- Turf.js for geographic distance helpers
- IndexedDB via Dexie
- PWA service worker
- Vitest for simulation tests
- GitHub Pages deployment

Important:
Game rules belong in pure TypeScript modules independent of React.

This keeps combat/economy testable and makes a later server-authoritative multiplayer version possible.

## 29. MVP boundary

The first playable build should include:
- world map;
- 40–60 cities;
- 15–25 resource sites;
- player + 5 AI factions;
- money/food/iron/oil;
- infantry/cavalry/artillery;
- movement;
- recruitment;
- city capture;
- morale;
- rebellion;
- city HP;
- barracks/depot/fortification/university;
- simple research;
- save/load;
- offline time advancement.

Do NOT begin by implementing aircraft, full diplomacy, 120 cities and 20 unit types.

Once the core loop is fun, expand the world data and late-game systems.

---

## 30. Quality bar

The game is ready for expansion only if:
- a player can understand why they are gaining/losing resources;
- a conquered city can realistically become a liability;
- supply lines matter;
- artillery has a real strategic use;
- fast units can exploit geography;
- a player can leave for two hours and return to an understandable world state;
- the UI remains usable on an iPhone screen;
- AI can finish a campaign without cheating resources.
